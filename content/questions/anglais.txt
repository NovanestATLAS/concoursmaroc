[ANG-001] grammar | beginner
Q: She ___ to school every day.
* goes
- go
- going
- is go
C: « goes ».
E: Présent simple, 3e personne du singulier : on ajoute -s ou -es au verbe (she goes). « Every day » indique une habitude.

[ANG-002] grammar | beginner
Q: I ___ my homework yesterday.
* did
- do
- have done
- was do
C: « did ».
E: « Yesterday » situe l'action dans un passé terminé : on emploie le prétérit (did). Le present perfect ne s'emploie pas avec un repère passé précis.

[ANG-003] grammar | intermediate
Q: She has lived in Rabat ___ 2015.
* since
- for
- from
- during
C: « since ».
E: « Since » + point de départ (since 2015) ; « for » + durée (for ten years). Avec le present perfect : She has lived here since 2015.

[ANG-004] grammar | intermediate
Q: If I ___ rich, I would travel around the world.
* were
- am
- will be
- would be
C: « were ».
E: Conditionnel de type 2 (hypothèse irréelle dans le présent) : If + prétérit, would + base verbale. En anglais soigné, on emploie « were » à toutes les personnes (« was » est courant à l'oral). « Will » et « would » ne s'emploient pas dans la subordonnée en « if ».

[ANG-005] grammar | beginner
Q: If it rains tomorrow, we ___ at home.
* will stay
- would stay
- stayed
- would have stayed
C: « will stay ».
E: Conditionnel de type 1 (situation possible) : If + présent simple, will + base verbale.

[ANG-006] grammar | advanced
Q: If you had studied harder, you ___ the exam last year.
* would have passed
- would pass
- will pass
- passed
C: « would have passed ».
E: Conditionnel de type 3 (hypothèse irréelle portant sur le passé) : If + past perfect, would have + participe passé. « Last year » situe le résultat dans le passé, ce qui exclut « would pass ».

[ANG-007] grammar | beginner
Q: Rabat is ___ capital of Morocco.
* the
- a
- an
- (no article)
C: « the ».
E: L'article défini « the » désigne un élément unique ou précisément identifié : un pays n'a qu'une capitale, d'où « the capital of Morocco ». « A/an » présenterait la capitale comme une parmi d'autres.

[ANG-008] grammar | beginner
Q: Choose the correct sentence.
* There are many students in the classroom.
- There is many students in the classroom.
- There are much students in the classroom.
- There have many students in the classroom.
C: « There are many students in the classroom. »
E: « There are » + nom pluriel. « Many » s'emploie avec les noms dénombrables (many students), « much » avec les indénombrables (much water).

- is signed
- has signed

E: Voix passive au prétérit : was/were + participe passé. « Last week » impose le passé.

[ANG-010] grammar | beginner

- is speaking
- spoke
C: « is spoken ».
E: Voix passive au présent simple : am/is/are + participe passé (speak → spoken).

[ANG-011] grammar | intermediate
Q: He is the man ___ car was stolen.
* whose
- who
- which
- whom
C: « whose ».
E: « Whose » exprime la possession (l'homme dont la voiture a été volée). « Who » renvoie à une personne sujet, « which » à une chose.

[ANG-012] grammar | intermediate
Q: When I arrived, they ___ dinner.
* were having
- have
- are having
- has had
C: « were having ».
E: Past continuous (was/were + V-ing) pour une action en cours au moment où une autre action passée (arrived) se produit.

[ANG-013] grammar | beginner

- more tall
- tallest
- the tallest
C: « taller ».
E: Comparatif des adjectifs courts : adjectif + -er + than. Avec les adjectifs longs : more + adjectif (more interesting).

[ANG-014] grammar | intermediate

- more interesting
- interestingest
- much interesting

E: Superlatif des adjectifs longs : the most + adjectif. « Ever » accompagne souvent le superlatif.

[ANG-015] grammar | beginner
Q: You ___ wear a seatbelt; it's the law.
* must
- might
- can
- would
C: « must ».
E: « Must » exprime l'obligation. « Might » exprime une faible probabilité et « can » la capacité ou la permission.

[ANG-016] grammar | intermediate
Q: I look forward to ___ from you.
* hearing
- hear
- heard
- be hearing
C: « hearing ».
E: Dans « look forward to », « to » est une préposition : elle est suivie de la forme en -ing. Formule très courante en fin de lettre ou d'e-mail.

[ANG-017] grammar | intermediate
Q: She is used to ___ early.
* getting up
- get up
- got up
- gets up
C: « getting up ».
E: « Be used to + V-ing » signifie « être habitué à ». À ne pas confondre avec « used to + base verbale », qui exprime une habitude passée révolue (I used to smoke).

[ANG-018] grammar | intermediate
Q: He asked me where ___.
* I lived
- did I live
- do I live
- I do live
C: « I lived ».
E: Au discours indirect, la question perd l'inversion et l'auxiliaire « do », et les temps reculent : « Where do you live? » devient « He asked me where I lived ».

[ANG-019] grammar | beginner
Q: How ___ money do you have?
* much
- many
- lot
- few
C: « much ».
E: « Money » est indénombrable : how much. On emploie « how many » avec un nom dénombrable au pluriel (how many books).

[ANG-020] grammar | intermediate
Q: By the time we got to the station, the train ___.
* had left
- has left
- leaves
- was leave
C: « had left ».
E: Le past perfect (had + participe passé) exprime une action antérieure à une autre action passée (got to the station).

[ANG-021] grammar | advanced
Q: Hardly ___ the house when it started to rain.
* had I left
- I had left
- I left
- have I left
C: « had I left ».
E: Après un adverbe restrictif placé en tête de phrase (hardly, scarcely, no sooner, never), on inverse le sujet et l'auxiliaire. « Hardly… when » exprime l'antériorité immédiate dans le passé et se construit avec le past perfect : Hardly had I left the house when it started to rain.

[ANG-028] vocabulary | intermediate

[ANG-029] vocabulary | intermediate
Q: Complete: "The company's profits ___ by 20% last year."
* increased
- raised
- grew up
- rose up
C: « increased ».
E: « Increase » est ici intransitif (les bénéfices ont augmenté). « Raise » est transitif (raise prices) ; « grow up » signifie grandir, pour une personne ; « rise » seul conviendrait (profits rose by 20%), mais pas « rise up », qui signifie se soulever ou s'élever.

[ANG-030] vocabulary | intermediate

- put
C: « make a decision ».
E: Collocations : make a decision, make a mistake, make money ; do homework, do business, do one's best. L'anglais britannique accepte aussi « take a decision ».

[ANG-031] vocabulary | beginner

[ANG-101] vocabulary | beginner
Q: Choose the word that best completes the sentence: "The lawyer charged a very high … for his services."
* fee
- fare
- debt
- hire
C: fee.
E: A fee is the money paid for professional services (a lawyer, a doctor, school fees). A fare is the price of a journey (a bus fare); a debt is money owed.

[ANG-102] vocabulary | intermediate
Q: Choose the word that best completes the sentence: "The sun was so strong that the curtains … from dark blue to light grey."
* faded
- fainted
- paled
- bleached
C: faded.
E: Colours fade when they lose their intensity over time. To faint means to lose consciousness; paled is used for faces; bleached suggests a deliberate chemical whitening.

[ANG-103] grammar | beginner
Q: Choose the correct option: "I'm looking forward to … you next week."
* seeing
- see
- to see
- have seen
C: seeing.
E: In "look forward to", "to" is a preposition, so it is followed by a gerund (-ing form): "I look forward to hearing from you."

[ANG-104] grammar | intermediate
Q: Choose the correct option: "Would you mind … the window? It's cold in here."
* closing
- to close
- close
- closed
C: closing.
E: "Would you mind" is always followed by the -ing form. The same applies to verbs such as enjoy, avoid, finish and suggest.

[ANG-105] grammar | beginner
Q: Choose the correct option: "His eyes were … tired that he couldn't read the text."
* so
- such
- too
- very
C: so.
E: "So + adjective + that" expresses a consequence. "Such" is used with a noun ("such a tiring day that…"); "too" would require "to" + infinitive ("too tired to read").

[ANG-106] grammar | intermediate
Q: Choose the correct option: "By the time we arrived at the station, the train …"
* had already left
- has already left
- already left
- was leaving already
C: had already left.
E: The past perfect describes an action completed before another past action ("we arrived"). The present perfect cannot be used with a past reference point.

[ANG-107] grammar | intermediate
Q: Choose the correct option: "If I … more time, I would learn another language."
* had
- have
- will have
- would have
C: had.
E: The second conditional (an imaginary present situation) uses "if" + past simple, then "would" + base verb. "Will" and "would" are not used in the if-clause.

[ANG-108] grammar | intermediate
Q: Choose the correct option: "She has been working for this company … 2019."
* since
- for
- during
- from
C: since.
E: "Since" introduces a starting point (since 2019, since Monday); "for" introduces a duration (for six years). Both are used with the present perfect.

[ANG-109] vocabulary | intermediate
Q: Choose the word that best completes the sentence: "The company needs to decide once and … what its position is."
* for all
- forever
- at all
- for good
C: "once and for all".
E: "Once and for all" is a fixed expression meaning "definitively, finally". "For good" also means permanently, but it is not used after "once and".

[ANG-110] vocabulary | beginner
Q: Choose the word that best completes the sentence: "Don't put your glass on the … of the table; it might fall."
* edge
- border
- boundary
- outside
C: edge.
E: The edge is the outer limit of an object (the edge of a table, of a cliff). A border separates countries; a boundary marks the limit of an area.

[ANG-111] vocabulary | intermediate
Q: Choose the word that best completes the sentence: "There's a problem with my car: one of the tyres is …"
* flat
- uneven
- depressed
- compressed
C: flat.
E: A flat tyre has lost its air. This collocation is the standard one; "depressed" describes a person's mood.

[ANG-112] grammar | intermediate
Q: Choose the correct option: "The report … by the manager before the meeting started."
* had been approved
- had approved
- has been approving
- was approving
C: had been approved.
E: The report does not act: the passive voice is needed (be + past participle). The past perfect passive shows that the approval happened before the meeting started.

[ANG-113] grammar | beginner
Q: Choose the correct option: "Neither the students nor the teacher … aware of the change."
* was
- were
- are
- have been
C: was.
E: With "neither… nor", the verb agrees with the nearest subject: "the teacher" is singular. With "Neither the teacher nor the students", we would say "were".

[ANG-114] vocabulary | intermediate
Q: Choose the word that best completes the sentence: "When there is a doubt, the examiner's decision is …"
* final
- certain
- fixed
- definite
C: final.
E: A decision that cannot be changed or appealed is "final" ("The referee's decision is final"). "Definite" means clear or certain, but it is not used in this fixed expression.

[ANG-115] reading | intermediate
Q: Read the sentence: "Teachers who are more open to new ideas tend to produce students with greater intellectual flexibility." It can be inferred that:
* Open-minded teaching helps students think more flexibly
- Strict teachers always produce the best students
- Students' flexibility does not depend on their teachers
- New ideas are harmful in the classroom
C: The teacher's openness supports the students' flexibility.
E: The sentence establishes a link between the teachers' openness and the students' intellectual flexibility. The other options contradict it or are not supported by it. Inference questions ask what the text implies, not what we think.

[ANG-116] reading | intermediate
Q: Read the sentence: "In a group of five, there are five people doing the same thing: every idea must be understood by four others." The main idea is that:
* Communication becomes more complex as the group grows
- Groups of five are always inefficient
- Only two people can really communicate
- Large groups make decisions faster
C: The more members, the more complex communication becomes.
E: The sentence compares a pair with a group of five to show that each message must reach and be understood by more people. It does not say that groups of five are inefficient, nor that large groups are faster.

[ANG-117] vocabulary | beginner
Q: Choose the word that best completes the sentence: "I'm sorry, I didn't … to disturb you."
* mean
- think
- suppose
- hope
C: mean.
E: "I didn't mean to…" expresses that something was not intentional. It is a very common way to apologise in English.

[ANG-118] grammar | advanced
Q: Choose the correct option: "Hardly … the room when the phone rang."
* had he entered
- he had entered
- did he enter
- he entered
C: "Hardly had he entered…"
E: When a negative adverb such as hardly, scarcely, no sooner or never begins the sentence, the subject and the auxiliary are inverted: "Hardly had he entered the room when the phone rang."

* are playing
- play
- plays
- played
C: are playing.

[ANG-203] grammar | beginner
Q: Choose the correct option: "I … my homework yesterday evening."
* did
- have done
- do
- was doing yesterday
C: did.
E: "Yesterday evening" is a finished time in the past: we use the past simple. The present perfect cannot be used with a finished past time expression.

[ANG-204] grammar | intermediate
Q: Choose the correct option: "I … in Rabat since 2020."
* have lived
- live
- lived
- am living
C: have lived.
E: "Since" + a starting point shows an action that began in the past and continues now: present perfect (or present perfect continuous: have been living).

[ANG-205] grammar | intermediate
Q: Choose the correct option: "While I … TV, the phone rang."
* was watching
- watched
- am watching
- have watched
C: was watching.
E: The past continuous describes an action in progress (watching TV) interrupted by a shorter action in the past simple (the phone rang).

[ANG-206] grammar | intermediate
Q: Choose the correct option: "Look at those clouds! It … rain."
* is going to
- will
- rains
- would
C: is going to.
E: "Be going to" is used for predictions based on present evidence (the clouds). "Will" is used for spontaneous decisions and general predictions.

[ANG-207] grammar | beginner

* the largest
- the larger
- largest
- more large
C: the largest.
E: The superlative of a short adjective is "the + adjective + -est". The comparative "larger than" compares two things.

[ANG-208] grammar | intermediate

* more difficult
- difficulter
- most difficult
- the more difficult
C: more difficult.
E: Long adjectives (two or more syllables, except those ending in -y) form the comparative with "more": more difficult, more expensive.

[ANG-209] grammar | intermediate
Q: Choose the correct option: "He is not … to drive a car."
* old enough
- enough old
- too old
- so old
C: old enough.
E: "Enough" comes after an adjective (old enough) but before a noun (enough money).

[ANG-210] grammar | intermediate
Q: Choose the correct option: "If it rains tomorrow, we … at home."

- would stay
- stayed
- stay will
C: will stay.

* would have passed
- would pass
- will pass
- had passed
C: would have passed.

- builds
- is building

- speaks
- is speaking
- spoke
C: is spoken.

- which
- whose
- whom
C: who.

[ANG-215] grammar | intermediate

* whose
- who
- which
- that
C: whose.
E: "Whose" expresses possession (his car was stolen). It can refer to people or things.

[ANG-216] grammar | intermediate

C: She said that she was tired.

- had seen

[ANG-219] grammar | intermediate
Q: Choose the correct option: "There isn't … milk left in the fridge."
* any
